Julia · Numerical linear algebra

30,720 numbers down to 616

I wanted to see how much of a noisy 3D array I could reconstruct from a much smaller set of numbers. In Julia, I tried two ways to choose the factors in a Tucker decomposition. Both stored 616 numbers. The difference was how much detail came back.

The idea came from an ENSEEIHT project I worked on with a teammate using the school’s materials. I built the public demo separately with generated data.

Hi, I’m Ahmed. I study maths and computer science at ENSEEIHT. One bit of maths I keep coming back to is approximation. If I can solve the smaller version of a problem, I want to know what’s left of the original and which details I’ve lost along the way. I like taking those questions from the page into a piece of code I can run and check.

In a first-year project, a teammate and I worked on compressing a three-dimensional array. I later made a small version of the experiment in Julia with generated data. It started with over 30,000 numbers. Both methods got that down to 616. What interested me was that they used the same amount of storage, but one reconstructed the input more accurately. That gave me a concrete way to talk about the trade-off.

I’d like to do more of that in scientific software. At EPFL, Daniel Kressner’s work gives me ideas for trying different ranks and measuring their cost. At Manchester, Nicholas Higham’s work makes me want to test what happens when the arithmetic gets less precise. Stanford’s Summer Session is another possibility. I’d like to learn more about matrix computation there and understand the algorithms behind the SVD that Gene Golub helped develop. The common thread in all three ideas is finding a question I can turn into an experiment and explain clearly to someone else.

Low-rank approximation

Daniel Kressner

Kressner works on low-rank methods for larger computing problems. My Julia demo uses one choice of rank. His work gives me a reason to try several and see where extra accuracy stops being worth the extra memory and time. EPFL research profile

Lausanne, Switzerland

EPFL exchange semester

I’d look for a numerical mathematics course and turn my Julia demo into a comparison across several ranks. An exchange would depend on an agreement and nomination through ENSEEIHT. EPFL exchange information

Numerical stability

Nicholas Higham

Higham’s work makes me want to ask the same question of a method on easy inputs and on difficult ones. Does the answer hold up when the input changes a little or the arithmetic gets less precise? Manchester research profile

Manchester, United Kingdom

Study abroad at Manchester

I’d look for numerical analysis modules and compare how the same method behaves on well-conditioned and ill-conditioned matrices. The modules I can take would depend on the programme. Manchester programme options

Matrix computation

Gene Golub

I used the SVD as a tool in Julia. Golub’s work on making it practical makes me curious about the steps hidden behind that one function call. Stanford biography

Stanford, United States

I’d look for an applied maths or computing course for visiting students and compare SVD implementations as the input grows. Course availability, funding, and eligibility would determine whether this is realistic. Stanford programme information
