Present. I’m Ahmed El Fanaoui, an engineering student at ENSEEIHT with a particular interest in applied mathematics. What I bring to a team is the ability to take a mathematical idea, implement it, and design a test that shows where it works. I like asking how much accuracy we need, how expensive it is to get, and whether someone else can reproduce the result.

Past. In a first-year scientific computing project, a teammate and I worked on low-rank tensor approximation. I later made a Julia demo with synthetic data to compare two versions of the method. In one seeded experiment, the smaller representation kept 616 numbers from an input of more than 30,000. The two methods used the same amount of storage but produced different reconstruction errors. For IDE7, I worked with a teammate on the compile-and-run workflow. There I saw how much a clear diagnostic helps a user understand what to fix.

Future. I want to deepen my understanding of numerical linear algebra and work on scientific software. Daniel Kressner’s low-rank research points toward exploring tensor compression at EPFL. Nicholas Higham’s work on numerical stability makes Manchester an interesting place to study how errors propagate. Gene Golub’s contributions to the SVD make Stanford Summer Session a third possibility for exploring matrix computation. In each case, my goal is to come back with an experiment I can explain, share, and improve.
